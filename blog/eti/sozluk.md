# Collection of Words 

## Inci

* The reason I started examining this word is somewhat personal. I occasionally watch MMA fights, and I was intrigued by Dagestan,a small Caucasian country that has produced a remarkable number of successful fighters in recent years. When I looked up the hometowns of Khabib Nurmagomedov and Islam Makhachev, I found that they were from Makhachkala. What caught my attention was that the suffix -qala is not of Russian origin but a word also present in Turkic languages (originally derived from Arabic), which made me curious about the full meaning of the city’s name. The city, which received its current name Makhachkala in 1921 after the Bolshevik revolutionary Makhach Dakhadaev, was originally known among the local population in the 1840s as Anzhi/Anji/Inzhi/Inji-Kala. For a Turkish speaker, the term Anzhi (and its variants) closely resembles the modern Turkish word inci (“pearl”), and indeed, Anzhi-Kala is translated as “Pearl Fortress.” Notably, the Kumyks,which is one of the largest ethnic groups in Dagestan,are themselves a Turkic people. Tracing the word even further back, the Turkish "inci" derives from Old Turkic "yinçü", which in turn originates from the Chinese word zhēn-zhū (珍珠), meaning “pearl".
* Anzhi/Anji/Inzhi/Inji/Inci ---> Yinçü ---> zhēn-zhū 珍珠
* Additionally, the name of Yinçü Bilge Kağan (also known as Zhenzhu Khan), a ruler of the Eastern Turkic Khaganate, is also derived from the same word.

## Deve

* Deve turkcede ingilizcedeki camel hayvanidir. Bu kelimenin geldigi orijinle alakali farkli teoriler bulunmakla beraber benim en cok ilgincime giden "tepe" sozcugunun fonetiginden tureyerek gelmesi teorisiydi. Buna gore, deve'nin horgucleri "tepe"leri andirdigindan, zamanla "deve" sozcugune evrilerek, hayvanin ismi haline gelmistir.
